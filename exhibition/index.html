00 / THE IDEA

A website can
ask something
of us.

In these three artworks, the visitor is not just an audience member.

My exhibition focuses on three net artworks that allow people to participate through the internet instead of simply looking at finished artworks online. The three projects began between 1994 and 2002, and each uses different website features to involve visitors.

In Douglas Davis's The World's First Collaborative Sentence, visitors add their own writing to a shared online sentence. Their contributions become part of the artwork, and the sentence continues to grow. In Martine Neddam's Mouchette, visitors explore the personal website of a fictional young girl. They can answer questions through online forms, making the website feel like a conversation with a real person. In Miranda July and Harrell Fletcher's Learning to Love You More, the artists posted creative assignments online. People from different places completed these tasks and submitted photographs, writing, or recordings to be shared on the website.

I chose these works because the internet plays an important role in how each artwork functions. The first depends on people adding text online, the second uses online communication, and the third brings together creative contributions from people around the world. Although they use different methods, all three show how visitors can help create or shape an artistic experience through the internet.

ACT TWO: RESPOND

Mouchette

MARTINE NEDDAM / 1996

This interactive website presents a fictional young girl named Mouchette. Visitors can explore her personal website and answer questions through online forms, creating an experience of communicating with a fictional person.

Here, clicking and answering are part of the experience.
